# Independent research playbook

**Goal:** corroborate or *challenge* every SME claim. You are not confirming the expert — you are pressure-testing them. The most valuable outputs are the corrections.

## Method
1. **Take each SME claim as a hypothesis to test**, not a fact to dress up.
2. **Search for the incumbent/competitor's real profile** — what it is, how it's sold, ownership, pricing, structure. Primary sources over aggregators.
3. **Mine review sites for buyer voice** — and for regional campaigns, the local review sites matter most (e.g. G2/Capterra in the US, OMR Reviews in DACH), plus neutral trade blogs. Translate as needed; flag translation.
4. **Separate on-thesis from off-thesis review content.** A finance buyer's complaint about consolidation is evidence; end-user portal rage (e.g. end-user login complaints about the incumbent) is NOT buyer-relevant and must be *excluded from copy* — note it so nobody "discovers" it later.
5. **Actively hunt contradictions.** Where does the SME's account not hold up? (In one campaign, three of the founder's load-bearing claims — what the incumbent could and couldn't do, and that "nothing sits between the incumbent and the enterprise suite" — were corrected by desk research.)

## Tiering findings
- **Confirmed** — SME corroborated by independent evidence.
- **Corrected** — SME directionally right but imprecise; research sharpened/fixed it. Use the corrected version. These are gold.
- **SME-asserted — unconfirmed** — the SME gave a specific account, usually with a mechanism, that research can neither confirm nor contradict. Stronger than Hypothesis, not Validated. Route by claim type, not reflexively to calls: capability claims (ours or a competitor's) go to product for written confirmation — buyers don't discuss ledger architecture on calls, so call recordings will never resolve them. Behavioral and pain claims go to call validation.
- **Hypothesis** — plausible SME claim research could NOT confirm, and with no specific mechanism behind it. Do not state as fact; route to call validation.

- **Rejected** — contradicted or unfindable; drop.

## Hard rules
- **Cite real URLs only.** Never fabricate a source or a deep link. If you can't retrieve a stable per-item URL, link the parent page and say so.
- **Copyright:** paraphrase; short quotes only; never reproduce large blocks.
- **Numbers need a source.** A market-share stat from one undated aggregator is *directional*, not a citable fact — label it so, and prefer the qualitative shape over false-precise decimals.
- **Flag single-source claims.** One site ≠ corroborated.

## Output
A research findings doc mapping each claim → tier → source(s) with links, plus a list of corrections and a list of "exclude from copy" items with reasons. This feeds the call worksheet (Stage 3) and the brief's research-sources callout.
