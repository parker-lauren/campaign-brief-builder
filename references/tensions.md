# Tension resolution

**Goal:** surface every conflict between sources, distinguish genuine judgment calls from data gaps, and route each to the right human. The process exists because SME brain-dumps, research, calls, product truth, and existing docs regularly disagree — and codifying a claim before resolving the disagreement is how a confident-sounding brief goes wrong.

## Find the tensions
After research + calls + customer evidence, look for every place two sources disagree:
- SME vs. research (SME said X; research shows Y).
- SME/research vs. calls (claimed pain buyers don't actually voice; claimed competitor buyers already eliminated).
- Claim vs. product truth (we assert a capability product says we don't have — the most dangerous kind).
- Brief vs. an existing standing document (a positioning doc that now conflicts).
- A claim that survived as a *conclusion* but had its *reason* invalidated.

## Sort them
- **Judgment calls** (sources genuinely conflict; a human must decide) — these need a person, not more data.
- **Data gaps** (no conflict, just missing) — these need gathering, not a decision.
- **Blocking vs. non-blocking** — does this stop a rep/asset from going out, or just sharpen it? Product-truth contradictions and misfiring rep claims are usually blocking.

## Route each to the right owner
- **Strategy/category/hook** → founder/strategy SME.
- **Deal reality / shortlist / win-loss / in-market ground truth** → in-market rep.
- **Capability boundary / integration / certification** → product — and keep the person off anything confidential to a former employer.
- **Conflicting standing doc** → whoever owns that doc.

## Hold them open, visibly
Put unresolved tensions in the brief as an explicit OPEN callout — never silently pick a side. Frame them as confirmations where you have a hypothesis ("confirming X") and open questions where you don't. When resolved, log the resolution in the changelog and update the affected sections + downstream assets.

## Prep the human
Produce clean, per-owner lists the user can walk into meetings with — one-sentence items, phrased as confirmations/questions, grouped by who answers them, with a note on what each answer unlocks in the brief.
