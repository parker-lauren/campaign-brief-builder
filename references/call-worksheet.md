# Call-validation worksheet

**Goal:** validate claims against real buyer calls and capture verbatim buyer language. The user runs the searches in their call-recording tool or call-transcript project and brings back annotated findings. You build the instrument; they run it; you fold results back in.

## Build the worksheet as a search instrument, not a dump
For each claim, provide:
- **The claim** and its current tier.
- **🔍 Call search prompt** — what to search for (content nouns: the topic, competitor, pain — not meta-words like "discussed"), and *what would confirm vs. challenge vs. sharpen it*.
- **A blank "Call findings" line** for the user to fill — including *absence* of evidence, which is itself signal.

Prioritize the claims that are Hypothesis or Corrected — those need the calls most. Mark the highest-priority ones (the load-bearing wedge, the spear) explicitly.

## What to tell the user to capture
- **Verbatim buyer phrases** — the single highest-value output; they become both the brief's buyer-language section and the actual copy. Over-collect. Source + date each.
- **Whether reps over-claimed and got corrected by a buyer** — that's a do-not-claim landmine made real.
- **Named accounts** to pull for reference/proof.
- **Absence** — "searched X, buyers never mention it" changes a claim's tier.

## Folding results back
- Update each claim's tier based on findings (promote Validated, cut Rejected, keep Hypothesis honest). Calls cannot promote an SME-asserted capability claim — absence of buyer discussion is not evidence either way on a product-architecture question. Leave those at SME-asserted and push them to product.

- Note whether each statement came from the buyer (Human turns) or a rep — a rep's pitch is not buyer validation.
- Separate deal evidence (calls) from market voice (public reviews) — different reliability; keep them labeled.
- Watch for claims that survived as *conclusions* but had their *reasons* invalidated (e.g. revenue pain real, but not blamed on the incumbent — reframe the wedge, don't drop the claim).

## Honesty
Some claims public research and calls simply can't confirm (behavioral assertions, thin-volume segments). The right outcome is sometimes "confirmed we can't validate this yet — stays Hypothesis or gets cut," not a manufactured citation.
